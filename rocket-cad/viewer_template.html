How it steers and sees

  • Four vanes sit in the fan's exhaust, each on its own small servo. Tilting them tips the rocket, and twisting them turns it.
  • The cameras are a stereo pair behind two small ports in the nose, looking forward, not up. A camera in the very tip would only see sky.
  • The rocket turns to face the way it is travelling, so one forward pair covers its path.
  • The GPS sits in the tip, where it has a clear view of the sky through the plastic.
  • Air enters through the ring gap on a checker row line and leaves through the tail.

Not done yet

  • The fan seat assumes a 98 mm housing, 80 mm long, and nothing stops the fan turning in its seat.
  • The servo mounts fit 9 g class servos. Whether those are strong enough for the vanes is untested.
  • The camera upright fits small 25 mm camera boards 50 mm apart. A full-size single-board computer does not fit in the nose.
  • The legs are thin hollow prints held by three screws each. Expect to replace one after a hard landing.
  • Nothing has been sliced, printed or flown.
